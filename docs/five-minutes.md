# The method in five minutes

*Write it in the daybook. Post what matters. Close the week.*

This is the whole method on one sheet: print it and keep it next to your notes. You need a folder of text files, or a notebook. Everything else in [the method](method.md) can wait until you need it.

## 1. Log: write on today's page

- One page a day, named by its date: `daybook/2026/11/2026-11-16.md`, or a notebook page with the date at the top.
- One short line per thing, with the time: `- 14:20 John says the budget needs sign-off`
- Don't decide where it belongs. Everything goes on today's page first.
- Once the day is over, the page is a record. Tick a task, fix a link or strike out what is no longer true; write anything else on today's page.

## 2. Mark: say what needs something

Four marks are enough to start. All are optional, because the close catches what you forget.

| Write | It is |
|---|---|
| `- [ ] Send the invoice (due 2026-11-20)` | A task, with a date if it has one. `[x]` when done, `[-]` when dropped |
| `- Should we take the train?` | A question: a line that ends in `?` |
| `- Decided: we go in July` | A decision |
| `- ! They may cancel the contract` | Important: something you want to see again |

## 3. Link and post: give what comes back a note

- When a person, a project or a topic comes up again, give it a note, `notes/jane-doe.md`, and write its name in double brackets on the lines about it: `- Lunch with [[jane-doe]]`
- When a line should also live on its note, such as a decision, a risk or a new fact, post it: copy it word for word onto the note, with a link to its day: `- Decided: we cut the mobile app ([[2026-11-10]])`
- Post what you will look for in the note. Leave the rest on its day, where search still finds it.

## 4. Close: once a week, in about fifteen minutes

1. Read the week's pages.
2. Every task that is overdue, or open for three weeks or more: **do** it, **date** it, make it **someday**, or **drop** it.
3. Every open question, and everything you wait for: **answer** it, **chase** it, or **drop** it.
4. Look at what comes before the next close: dates, events, birthdays.
5. Post what matters, and give a note to what keeps coming back.

A skipped week costs nothing. Don't go back to it: close this week, and old tasks come up by the three-week rule.

## 5. Find: by name, link or words

- Open a thing's note, then the lines that link to it: its history, day by day. A search for `[[jane-doe` finds them.
- Search the folder for a word: `- [ ]` finds the open tasks, `Decided:` the decisions.
- You never need to remember where you put something.

**On paper**, the steps are the same. A card per thing stands in for a note, posting is copying the line onto its card with its date, and the close is the week's pages and a pen. What paper cannot do is search.
